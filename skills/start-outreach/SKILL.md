# Start outreach to these people

PoliteReach sends only text that was written and stored beforehand. Write every word now, show it, and send nothing without the user's yes. That one yes to the preview is the launch: the confirmed import starts sending. Never ask for a second yes to launch.

## Before you start

- Load the user's outreach skill (their voice, ICPs, proof, banned words). If they have none, offer the `outreach-skill-builder` skill first; otherwise ask for the offer and voice before writing.
- An outreach skill built before 2026-11-20 may say to stage with `launch: false` after the yes. That step is superseded: launch on the yes unless the user asked to hold it.
- `li_list_accounts`: pick the sending account. With more than one account, every write must name it (`sessionLabel`). Never guess.
- Note the account tier: only a Premium account sends connection notes. A free account drops them.
- Check `inviteNotes` in `li_list_accounts`. Premium can also have a monthly note allowance. When `state` is `spent` (or `not_sent_on_free`), write no connection notes; `guidance` says until when: LinkedIn will not deliver them. The invites still go out, and messages are unaffected.

## Steps

0. **No list yet?** `li_search_leads` finds people who fit an ICP from LinkedIn's filters (titles, seniority, function, location, industry, headcount, companies, keywords). 25 lookups per page of up to 25 that returns anyone (a page of 3 costs the same; nobody found is free): run it with `estimateOnly` first. Put every target company (up to 50), title and place in ONE call: each list matches any of its entries. Never one call per company; each call pays a whole page for a handful of people. Results are marked `alreadyContact` / `doNotContact`; drop those. Their links are search ids (`/in/ACwAA…`). Pass them to research, checks and import as they are: every tool resolves each to the public profile URL first and says so in `searchIds`. An id it could not resolve is not imported; relay its `reason`.
1. **Research the whole list in one call.** `li_research_profiles` with every `profileUrl` (up to 500), `include` `pipeline`, `profile`, `company`, plus `posts` when the first touch may be about a post. Run `estimateOnly: true` first on a big list and show the cost. It returns one row per person: every campaign they are already in, who they are, their employer, their latest posts. Someone already in the pipeline is reused free. A list that takes longer than about 20 seconds returns a `jobId`: read it with `li_job_result` (`jobId`, `waitMs`, then `offset` = `nextOffset` until it is null). Several separate lists: research them in parallel calls, not one after another. Do not rebuild this from `li_check_targets`, `li_enrich_profiles` and `li_fetch_posts` joined by URL.
   - Over about 25 people: triage first with `fields: [name, role, company, industry, size, location, inPipeline, postUrl]` (add `campaigns` to see which campaigns) on `li_research_profiles` or `li_job_result`: one flat row per person. Then read the full rows (no `fields`) only for the people you will write to.
   - Flag to the user before going further: anyone with `pipeline.memberships` (already in a campaign; `hasInbound` = mid-conversation; `inPipeline` in the flat row), and anyone whose profile is the sending account itself (compare with `profileSlug` from `li_list_accounts`).
   - `pipeline.unreadable` (`inPipeline: null` + `pipelineUnreadable` in the flat row), or a `status` of `failed`, `pending` or `provider_limit`, means not known, never "none". `provider_limit` = our data provider is at its monthly usage limit: tell the user, research those people again later, never treat it as `no_posts` or `not_found`. Before importing someone whose posts are `provider_limit`, offer to wait and research them again rather than silently dropping the engage step.
   - For one person's stored notes and conversation, `li_contact_dossier`.
2. **Company detail only when needed.** `li_enrich_company` when a first line needs a specific, recent detail. Never an outside scraper.
3. **Pick a post.** From the research `posts`: ONE recent post that is their own and worth reacting to; the first touch can then be about something they actually said. Only a post with `reactable: true` can be an `engagePostUrl` (a group post is not). A flat row's `postUrl` is only a rule-picked candidate (newest reactable post that is not a repost): read the full row's posts for the text before choosing. `no_posts` means nothing recent, `unreadable` means it could not tell: either way write as usual, about no post.
4. **Match each person to one ICP.** If none fits, say so and suggest who to target instead. Never force a message.
5. **Write the whole sequence.** Connection note (Premium only, and not while `inviteNotes.state` is `spent`), first message, then follow-ups and a close: at most four messages in all. Follow the user's skill; default rules:
   - Three short sentences at most; follow-ups two short lines, each adding one new reason.
   - No link in the first message, and never the booking link in any sequence message: they send by themselves and PoliteReach reads no calendar. Make the ask from "The ask" in the user's outreach skill; a booking link goes only in the reply to their yes. End on a concrete ask. The close is a yes or no question.
   - No em dashes, exclamation marks, emojis or filler. Never invent a client, number, price or date.
   - Names in all capitals ("SOHAN SINGH") or all lowercase get normal capitals ("Sohan"; "MARY-ANNE O'BRIEN" → "Mary-Anne O'Brien"). A mixed-case name ("McDonald", "deVries", "AJ DeVito") is the person's own spelling: keep it. Prefer `{first}` in `defaultSteps` over typing the name into each row: it applies this fix for you.
6. **Pick the campaigns.** `li_list_campaigns` first, compact: `namePrefix` and `sessionLabel` to narrow it, `fields: ["name", "accountLabel", "contactCount", "launchedAt"]`, and `nextOffset` as `offset` while `truncated`; `status: "not_launched"` lists only campaigns that send nothing yet. Note each campaign's account, exact name and `launchedAt`. A campaign that is already launched sends whatever is imported into it; `launch: false` cannot stage people there. When the user asked to stage or hold the batch, use a new campaign name (with HOLD in it if they want it held indefinitely, see step 10). To add to an existing campaign, pass its `campaignId`: a name that is not an exact match creates a second campaign.
7. **Pick the cadence.** `li_list_strategies`; use one whose steps and delays fit. If none fits, preview `li_create_strategy` (at most 4 message steps) and create it with `confirmCreate: true` only on a yes. Fill only the fields the strategy's steps need: a 3-step strategy (first, followup_1, closer) takes `firstMessage`, `followUp1` and `closer`.
8. **Preview.** `li_ingest_prospects` with `confirmIngest: false` (no `launch`, so the confirmed call is identical), the account, the strategy and ICP named explicitly (`strategyName` / `icpName`, or `strategyId` / `icpId`), the campaign (`campaignId`, or a new `campaignName`), and each prospect's `connectionNote`, `firstMessage`, `followUp1`..`followUp3` / `closer`.
   - **Many campaigns, ONE call.** A list split by ICP, account or lane goes in one call of up to 500 rows: put `sessionLabel`, `campaignName` (or `campaignId`), `strategyName` and `icpName` on each row; a row leaving one out takes the call's. Never one call per campaign. The preview's `campaigns` lists each campaign it will fill, with its account and count.
   - **Shared copy.** When the follow-ups are the same for everyone, send them once as `defaultSteps` (`firstMessage`, `followUp1`..`followUp3`, `closer`, `connectionNote`) and keep only the personal lines on each row. `{first}` is the only variable (the row's `firstName`, else the first word of `name`). Read `copy`: it lists every row over a limit (a note over 300 is shortened) and any row refused for having no name.
   - Fill `location` and `timezone` (IANA zone from the location: city if known, else the country's main zone; blank if unknown) on every row. Accounts can send in each prospect's local hours, and that needs the zone.
   - Store the research too, so later drafts reuse it: `name`, `headline`, `role`, `company`, `companyDomain`, `icpBucket`, `whyTheyFit`, `whatTheySell`, `context`.
   - `startStage`: `connection` (default) invites first. Someone already connected to that account gets `message`: their first message is scheduled with no invite. Never `connection` for a connection. For someone with a chosen post, add `engagePostUrl` (and `engageReaction` if not a Like): the account reacts to that post first and the invite follows about a day later, in the person's own morning window (or the account's sending hours; 18-30 hours with no window to aim at). A reaction that cannot run releases the invite at once, so never write copy that only works if the reaction happened. The preview echoes none of the text and returns no per-person checks, only `prospectCount`: print each person's note and messages from your own draft. Who was already approached comes back only on the confirmed call.
   - Show the preview with one line saying that a yes starts sending: the confirmed import launches it.
9. **Launch on the yes.** Same call with `confirmIngest: true` and an `idempotencyKey` (any unique string), WITHOUT `launch: false`: `launch` defaults to true, so the confirmed import launches every campaign it fills and invites start going out under the account's pace. After a timeout, 502 or 503, call again with the SAME key: a call that finished returns its stored answer instead of importing twice. Then relay:
   - `campaigns`: each campaign touched, created or existing, with imported, updated and rejected counts. `routed` rows not imported carry the reason.
   - `collisions`: people already approached from the user's accounts (information, not a refusal).
   - `suppressed`: people on the do-not-contact list, not imported.
   - Read back `result.campaignName`, `result.campaignCreated` and `result.campaignLaunchedAt`, then tell the user it is live and what goes out first: the first invites (or, for `startStage: "message"` people, their first messages) under the account's pace, never a promised date. Rows that landed in the wrong campaign: `li_move_contact` (same account only), and tell the user.
10. **Only when the user asked to stage it.** "Stage it", "hold it", "don't start yet": pass `launch: false` on both calls, into a campaign that is not launched (`launch: false` holds nothing in a live one; `result.warnings` says so). Then say plainly that it is NOT sending, and that it starts with `li_launch_campaign`. If they want it held indefinitely, put HOLD in the campaign name: the account owner's reminder emails about unlaunched campaigns skip it. Otherwise tell them they will get a reminder email about it a day later. When they say start: `li_launch_campaign` with that `campaignId` (several: ONE call with `campaignIds` from step 9's `campaigns`); without `confirmLaunch` it lists them with their people and anything it would refuse, then the same call with `confirmLaunch: true` launches each. A launch starts everyone staged in the campaign: warn first if it holds other staged people.

## Testing strategies against each other

Never deal people into lanes by hand. `li_experiment_assign` does it on the server:
- First call: `experiment` with a `name`, 2-8 `arms` (`key`, `strategyName`, `engage`), optionally `requirePostForArms` and a `controlArm` for people without a reactable post. Later calls: `experimentId`. People in an arm stay there.
- With any engage arm, arms are also balanced by who has a reactable post (default). Someone in an engage arm with no post is invited WITHOUT a reaction and the reply warns; to avoid that, list the arm in `requirePostForArms` with a `controlArm`.
- Each row carries its account and ICP (or the call's), its own text, and `engagePostUrl` when it has a reactable post. Shared text per arm goes in `armSteps`, shared text for every arm in `defaultSteps`.
- Preview with `confirmAssign: false` (shows the split per ICP and account and the campaigns), then on the user's yes `confirmAssign: true` with an `idempotencyKey`: that launches every arm's campaign. Only when the user asked to stage the test, pass `launch: false`, say it is NOT sending, and start it later with `li_launch_campaign` on the reply's `launch.campaignIds`. Each campaign is named after the experiment, so to hold it indefinitely put HOLD in the experiment name; otherwise say a reminder email comes a day later.
- Results: `li_outreach_analytics` with `groupBy: "arm"` and `experimentId`: acceptance, reply, booking and `positiveReply` per arm. Quote each rate with its `n` and interval; small arms decide nothing.

## Fixing a staged batch

- A new lane with nobody in it yet: `li_create_campaign` with no targets creates an empty campaign. With targets it launches on `confirmCreate: true` unless you pass `launch: false` (only when the user asked to stage it; HOLD in the name as in step 10).
- A staged campaign on the wrong strategy: `li_edit_campaign` with `strategyId` and `resnapshot: true` (`dryRun` first). Works only while nobody in it has been invited or sent anything; it names anyone missing text for a new step.
- People in the wrong lane: `li_move_contact` (same account only), with `resnapshot: true` to take the target campaign's steps.

## After launch

- Invites drip inside the account's own pace (from `li_list_accounts`, `invitePace`). That pace is PoliteReach's safety policy, not LinkedIn's, and is not a setting. Never promise a date. After LinkedIn refuses with its own weekly limit, the account's weekly ceiling is lowered for a while (`learnedWeeklyCap`).
- `invites.backlogWarning` (`li_account_activity`) is a warning only: many invites are pending on LinkedIn, and new invites still go out. If `autoWithdraw.enabled` is false, suggest the account holder turn on `li_set_auto_withdraw`. If it is on but `autoWithdraw.untracked` is false, it withdraws only invites PoliteReach sent; invites sent by hand stay unless they turn on `untracked`. Never switch either on for them.
- Messages start once each person accepts, inside the account's sending hours.
- To change or hold queued text later, use the `queued-messages` skill.
